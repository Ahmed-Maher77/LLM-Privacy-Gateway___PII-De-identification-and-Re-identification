[09:30:04] PROF. HELENA LINDQVIST:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Good morning, everyone. Thank you for joining this consultation.
The purpose today is to review the proposed Phase III protocol for
BX-4471, our investigational monoclonal antibody targeting HER2-low
metastatic breast cancer. Rajiv will circulate the draft protocol
document after this meeting. I'd like to begin with Dr. Adeyemi
because the proposed trial sites in West Africa raise important
ethical and logistical questions.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:31:22] DR. SAMUEL ADEYEMI:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Thank you, Professor. I have three concerns. First, the protocol
requires tumor biopsies at screening and again at week 12. In our
setting, that is not always feasible — many of our patients travel
over 400 kilometers to reach the Lagos University Teaching Hospital.
Second, the washout period of 14 days is too short for patients
who have been on tamoxifen. Third, and most importantly, the
informed consent form is currently only available in English and
French. We serve Yoruba-, Hausa-, and Igbo-speaking populations.
This is a serious gap under the Declaration of Helsinki.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:33:15] PROF. HELENA LINDQVIST:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
That's a critical point. We will need translated consent forms
before any site activation in Nigeria. Mei-Ling, from a statistical
perspective, does the sample size calculation account for
anticipated dropout in these regions?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:33:48] DR. MEI-LING CHEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
It does not, Professor. The current power calculation assumes a
15% dropout rate across all sites. Based on historical data from
the KEYNOTE-355 and DESTINY-Breast04 trials, dropout in
resource-limited settings can reach 28-32%. If we don't adjust,
we risk underpowering the primary endpoint. I recommend
stratifying randomization by region and increasing the target
enrollment from 640 to 780 patients.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:35:10] MR. JAVIER ORTEGA:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
I'd like to flag a regulatory issue. If we increase enrollment
and modify the stratification, this becomes a substantial
modification under Article 19 of Regulation (EU) No 536/2014.
The EMA will require a new Part II submission via the Clinical
Trials Information System. That adds at least 60 days to the
timeline. Also — and this is important — the current protocol
cites ICH E6(R2), but the guideline has been updated to
ICH E6(R3). The ethics committee at Karolinska will reject
the submission if we don't reference the current version.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:37:02] PROF. HELENA LINDQVIST:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Noted. Rajiv, please add that to the action log. Ms. Al-Rashid,
you've been quiet. I want to hear the patient perspective.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:37:20] MS. FATIMA AL-RASHID:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Thank you, Professor. I have reviewed the protocol from the
patient's standpoint, and I have significant concerns. The
quality-of-life questionnaire is the EORTC QLQ-C30, which
has been validated in over 40 languages, but not in Arabic.
If you intend to enroll patients in the Gulf region, you
need the Arabic-validated version. Furthermore, the schedule
of assessments requires clinic visits every two weeks for the
first three months. For patients in rural areas of the UAE
or Saudi Arabia, that is a significant burden. I would
recommend allowing telehealth check-ins for weeks 2, 4, and 6,
with in-person visits at weeks 8 and 12.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:39:44] DR. SAMUEL ADEYEMI:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
I strongly support that. In fact, we have been using a
telehealth model at LUTH since 2021 with good results.
I would also add that the protocol should include a
provision for community health workers to conduct home
visits. This is standard practice in our setting.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:40:55] DR. MEI-LING CHEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
I want to caution against that from a data integrity
perspective. If assessments are conducted by different
personnel — nurses in Boston, community health workers
in Lagos, telehealth coordinators in Dubai — we introduce
inter-rater variability. We would need to implement
a rigorous training and certification program, and
possibly a centralized imaging review. I suggest we
include a blinded independent central review,
similar to what was done in the CLEOPATRA trial.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[09:42:30] MR. JAVIER ORTEGA:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Agreed. And under the new ICH E6(R3), the sponsor must
document a risk-based quality management plan. The
central review would need to be described in that plan.
I can share a template that was used in a recent
oncology submission to the UK MHRA.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

=====================================================================
INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT
=====================================================================
Interview Title:    Post-Incident Review — Data Breach at Meridian Health
Interview ID:       PIR-2024-0088
Date:              May 22, 2024
Time:              14:30 – 16:00 AEST
Location:          Sydney, Australia
Interviewer:       James O'Connor (Chief Information Security Officer,
                   Meridian Health Australia)
Interviewee:       Dr. Yuki Tanaka-O'Brien (Head of Clinical Informatics,
                   Meridian Health Australia)
Observer:          Amara Nwosu (External Advisor, Deloitte Cyber)
=====================================================================

[14:30:15] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Thank you for joining, Yuki. Amara from Deloitte is here as an
external observer. We're conducting this post-incident review
to understand how the ransomware attack on April 3rd compromised
our patient records system. Everything discussed today will be
included in the report to the Office of the Australian Information
Commissioner.

[14:31:02] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Thank you, James. Before we begin, I want to note that I've
already provided a written statement to our legal counsel at
MinterEllison. I'm happy to elaborate on anything in that
statement.

[14:31:30] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Dr. Tanaka-O'Brien, can you walk us through what you observed
on the morning of April 3rd?

[14:31:48] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
At approximately 8:15 AM, our clinical staff at the Royal
Prince Alfred Hospital reported that they couldn't access
the electronic medical records system. I contacted our IT
operations center, which is managed by a vendor called
Nexus IT Services. Their on-call engineer, a gentleman named
Marcus Chen, confirmed that multiple servers had been
encrypted.

[14:32:35] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
When you say "Nexus IT Services," is that the same Nexus
that we contracted with in 2019, or the Nexus that was
acquired by Telstra in 2021?

[14:32:52] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
It's the same company, but after the Telstra acquisition it
was rebranded as "Nexus Health Solutions." Same staff, same
contracts. Marcus Chen stayed on through the transition.

[14:33:20] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What was the initial scope of the breach? How many patient
records were affected?

[14:33:35] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The forensic report from CrowdStrike estimated approximately
4.7 million patient records were exfiltrated. That includes
patients at Royal Prince Alfred, St Vincent's Hospital, and
our clinics in Parramatta and Blacktown.

[14:34:10] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Were the records encrypted at rest?

[14:34:18] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, using AES-256. But the attackers compromised an
administrative account — I believe it belonged to a
contractor named Priya Sharma — and used her credentials
to access the decryption keys.

[14:34:50] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Was Ms. Sharma an employee of Meridian Health or of Nexus?

[14:35:00] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
She was a contractor through Nexus Health Solutions, but
she worked on-site at our Sydney office. Her manager was
David Kim, who reports to me.

[14:35:28] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Has Ms. Sharma been interviewed?

[14:35:35] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
She has. She's cooperating fully. Her account was accessed
from an IP address in Bucharest, Romania — which is
obviously not where she was. She was in Melbourne at the
time, attending a conference at the Melbourne Convention
Centre.

[14:36:10] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What about the threat actor? Has there been any attribution?

[14:36:22] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The Australian Federal Police and the FBI are jointly
investigating. The ransomware strain has been linked to a
group called BlackCat, also known as ALPHV. They're believed
to operate out of Russia. There's also speculation about a
connection to a North Korean group called Lazarus, but that's
unconfirmed.

[14:37:00] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
What was the ransom demand?

[14:37:08] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
$15 million USD in Monero. We did not pay. Our board made
that decision on April 5th, following advice from our
insurer, Marsh McLennan.

[14:37:35] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
How did the attackers initially gain access? Was it phishing,
a vulnerability, or something else?

[14:37:48] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
CrowdStrike's report indicates it was a vulnerability in
our VPN appliance — specifically a Fortinet FortiGate
device that hadn't been patched since January. The CVE
was CVE-2024-21762. Our patching team, led by a gentleman
named Tom Blackwood, had flagged it, but the remediation
ticket was deprioritized.

[14:38:30] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Who deprioritized it?

[14:38:38] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
That's a sensitive question. The ticket was managed through
our ServiceNow instance. The change advisory board — chaired
at the time by Rebecca Lindstrom — made the call. But I want
to be clear that Rebecca is no longer with the organization.
She left in February.

[14:39:15] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Was there any indication that the deprioritization was
deliberate?

[14:39:25] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
I have no evidence of that. I want to be careful not to
speculate. What I can say is that the same patch had been
applied at our Brisbane and Perth sites without issue.

[14:39:55] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Let's talk about the notification process. When did we
first notify affected patients?

[14:40:05] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
We notified the OAIC within 72 hours, as required under the
Privacy Act 1988. Patient notifications went out starting
April 15th, in batches. We used a vendor called
Experian Data Breach Resolution to handle the mail merge
and call center. The notification was signed by our CEO,
Michael Zhang.

[14:40:50] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Did you offer credit monitoring?

[14:40:58] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes. Twelve months of credit monitoring through Equifax
for all affected patients. The cost was approximately
$8.3 million AUD. Our CFO, Helen Papadopoulos, negotiated
the rate directly with Equifax.

[14:41:35] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Where are we now with remediation?

[14:41:45] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
We've engaged KPMG to conduct a full security assessment.
We've also implemented mandatory MFA across all
administrative accounts, and we're migrating our
infrastructure to Microsoft Azure with a zero-trust
architecture. The target completion date is September 30th.

[14:42:30] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
One more question. Is there anything you wish you'd done
differently?

[14:42:40] DR. YUKI TANAKA-O'BRIEN:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
I wish we'd listened to Tom Blackwood's team earlier. I
wish we'd had better visibility into our third-party
contractors. And I wish we'd encrypted our backup
infrastructure — the attackers were able to delete our
on-premise backups at the Sydney data center before we
could isolate them. We've since moved backups to AWS S3
with immutable retention.

[14:43:30] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Thank you, Yuki. Amara, any final questions?

[14:43:38] AMARA NWOSU:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
No, I think we have what we need. Thank you.

[14:43:45] JAMES O'CONNOR:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
This concludes the post-incident review. Recording stopped.

[14:43:50] END OF TRANSCRIPT
